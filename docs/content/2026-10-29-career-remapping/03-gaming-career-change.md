# After a Gaming Layoff: How to Find Your Next Role

If you have spent years in games, starting a job search can feel like learning a new system while the ground is moving. You may want another studio role, work in a different industry, or a direction that uses your experience in a new way.

Start with the work you performed. Translate it for someone who has never shipped a game. Then compare that work with current roles and build a small example of a capability you still need to demonstrate.

## 1. Recognize the disruption without assuming one cause

GDC's 2026 survey of more than 2,300 industry professionals reported that 28% of respondents had experienced a layoff in the previous two years. It also found substantial differences in AI use across roles and organizations. Those findings describe respondents' experiences; they do not establish that AI caused every layoff or that an entire discipline has disappeared. [Source: GDC's report summary](https://gdconf.com/article/gdc-2026-state-of-the-game-industry-reveals-impact-of-layoffs-generative-ai-and-more/).

Your next step can be practical even when the wider market is uncertain. Write down the work you want to keep doing, the constraints a new role must meet, and the kinds of work you want to leave behind. You are allowed to value your experience without wanting the same working environment again.

Keep two searches visible initially: roles in games that meet your requirements and roles elsewhere that use comparable capabilities. Compare the actual work before deciding that one path is the only option.

## 2. Translate studio language into work other teams recognize

A hiring manager outside games may not understand your pipeline, credits, or internal terminology. Explain the problem you handled, the decisions you made, and the output someone else relied on.

These are illustrative translations, not automatic qualifications:

| Games work | Transferable capability | Directions to investigate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reproduced gameplay bugs and verified fixes | Test design, defect reporting, regression judgment | Software QA, product QA, AI-output evaluation work |
| Maintained asset consistency across a pipeline | Visual review, constraints, handoff quality | Design production, creative operations, design systems support |
| Coordinated milestones and dependencies | Planning, risk communication, delivery coordination | Project coordination, product operations, implementation |
| Analyzed player behavior or live-ops results | Measurement, data validation, experiment interpretation | Product analytics, reporting operations, analytics quality |
| Planned and reviewed campaign assets | Audience judgment, briefing, channel execution | Marketing operations, lifecycle marketing, creative workflow work |
| Designed interactions and revised them after playtests | Prototyping, feedback synthesis, tradeoffs | UX or product design, subject to the role's specific requirements |

For QA, the functional overlap has a useful external reference: O*NET describes software QA work in terms of testing, documenting defects, and reporting problems. That supports investigating the connection; it does not establish readiness for every QA vacancy. [Source: O*NET software QA occupation](https://www.onetonline.org/link/details/15-1253.00).

Preserve your original title. Add a plain-language explanation of responsibilities where needed. “Game producer” does not automatically become “AI product manager,” and manual testing does not automatically establish automation engineering.

## 3. Check the gap before buying the transition story

Choose two directions and inspect current employer descriptions. For each, record recurring tasks, required tools, domain knowledge, seniority, location, and the evidence requested in the hiring process.

A games analyst may already understand measurement but need experience with a different data model. A designer may have strong critique skills but need to demonstrate accessible product interfaces. A producer may understand delivery while lacking a target role's technical implementation experience.

Mark each requirement as supported by past work, supported by a recent example, or unassessed. “Unassessed” means you need more information. It should not become an invented skill or a declaration that you cannot learn it.

Also inspect employment terms on the exact listing. Temporary labeling work, contract evaluation, product operations, and engineering roles can have very different conditions. A shared AI label does not make them interchangeable.

## 4. Build one example that connects your history to new work

Use synthetic or public material when former studio work is confidential. A fresh project can demonstrate a process without exposing unreleased assets, internal metrics, player data, or private tools.

**For QA:** create a small checker for fictional support replies. Define required fields, prohibited promises, and escalation rules. Keep development examples separate from evaluation examples. Save the actual report and explain a failure. If you use saved replies, label them synthetic; you have demonstrated checker mechanics, not live-model quality.

**For design:** choose one constrained interface or creative-production task. Define the audience, accessibility requirements, visual rules, and review rubric. Show source files, rejected alternatives, and the changes you made after review. Explain where AI assisted and which decisions remained yours.

**For production or product work:** prototype one handoff or planning workflow with a clear user and acceptance criteria. Demonstrate the normal path, a missing-input case, and how a person corrects an error. A local prototype should be described as a local prototype.

**For analytics:** use a synthetic dataset and a written metric definition. Create a report, check its calculations against the source, and show how you detect a misleading generated summary. Keep observed results separate from possible business benefits.

**For marketing:** build a brief-to-draft workflow with explicit audience and brand constraints. Include a review stage, a factual check, and examples that should be rejected. Draft quality does not establish campaign performance.

Choose the example closest to the work you want. The [portfolio guide](https://www.myaiskilltutor.com/learn/how-to-build-an-ai-portfolio) explains how to package files, decisions, checks, and limitations into a readable case study.

## 5. Restart the job search with a small operating system

If you have not searched for work in years, begin with a simple record. For each opportunity, save the exact employer link, date checked, role requirements, why it fits, next action, and outcome. Verify that the vacancy remains current before applying.

Create one resume per direction rather than rewriting your identity for every title. Keep a separate bank of accurate examples so tailoring does not become invention. Link the most relevant work sample and explain your contribution in a sentence.

Reconnect with people through a specific question. For instance: “I used to design regression cases for games and am exploring software QA outside studios. I built this small example. Which part of the workflow would a team need me to demonstrate next?” Adapt the question to the relationship; do not send a mass request for referrals.

Apply while you learn when the role is already a credible fit. A project can improve the conversation, but endless polishing can delay useful market feedback.

## 6. Prepare a career-change explanation you can stand behind

Use three parts: your existing strength, the new direction, and the evidence connecting them.

An illustrative QA explanation might be:

> My background is in games QA, where I reproduced defects and maintained repeatable checks. I am exploring software quality work that includes AI-assisted workflows. My recent independent project checks synthetic support replies against explicit rules. I can walk through the cases, a failure, and what I would need to test before using it in production.

That leaves room for honest questions. It does not require claiming that you were already an AI specialist.

Practice explaining the project without relying on the model to answer for you. Be ready to show how you inspect an unexpected output, change a requirement, and decide when to ask for help. These are concrete ways to demonstrate ownership.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to leave games to find work?**

No. Compare current opportunities inside and outside games against your interests and constraints. The article offers a way to investigate options, not a recommendation that everyone leave.

**Can games QA experience transfer to AI evaluation?**

Some capabilities may transfer, including case design and failure documentation. Check the target role's domain, scripting, research, and evaluation requirements. A small project can help test the connection.

**What if my portfolio is under an NDA?**

Keep restricted material private. Describe permitted responsibilities and create a fresh example using material you are allowed to share. Do not disguise confidential work as a public sample.

**Should I become a prompt engineer?**

Evaluate the responsibilities behind any title. Prompting can be one part of a larger workflow, but a title alone does not tell you the requirements, employment terms, or prospects.

**What should I do first if I have not applied for a job in years?**

Choose one direction, read current employer descriptions, create a task-based experience inventory, and update a focused resume. Begin a manageable application and conversation routine while closing one important evidence gap.
